## Contributing

This is an open and unfinished inquiry, and we welcome contribution in every
form — fresh insight, counter-argument, discussion, correction, revision, and
extension. Dissent is as welcome as agreement: a careful objection advances the
work as much as a new chapter does.

The series thinks about intimate life from a deliberately wide theoretical
range — the gift and its giving, the language of poetry, the practice of a
"diplomacy" of the external relational field, ritual and etiquette, the ethics
of technological mediation, the structure of rights, and more. We draw on, and
invite work from, many traditions without privileging any: political economy,
ethics, the theory of justice, normative theory, relational ontology,
psychology, philosophy, psychoanalysis, quantitative social science,
linguistics, feminist theory, and the formal sciences — theoretical physics and
field theory, algebraic topology, and algebraic geometry among them. The list is
open, not a boundary; if your field is not named here, take that as an
invitation, not an exclusion.

Our working conviction is that no single discipline commands the whole of the
reason these questions require. Each illuminates part of the matter and is
silent on the rest; the aim of gathering them is not to crown one but to let
them correct and extend one another — to develop understanding together.

**How to take part** — the approach:

1. **What you may do freely.** Read, copy, and make **non-commercial**
   derivatives ("extensions"), as long as you keep the attribution and license
   above.

2. **The carry-forward rule.** Any copy or extension must keep
   [`CONTRIBUTORS.md`](CONTRIBUTORS.md) and reproduce the contributor list as it
   stood in the version you built on — *the contributor list at the time of
   extension*. You may append yourself; you may **not** remove or alter existing
   entries. Every extension must also credit **Serendip Commons Society**.

3. **How to propose a contribution.**
   - Fork the repository and create a branch.
   - Make your change in [`book_src/`](book_src/), following the house
     conventions; the paper should still build cleanly with `make`.
   - Append yourself to `CONTRIBUTORS.md` under "Authors and contributors" with
     your name, the date, and a short note on what you changed.
   - Open a pull request (or send a patch to the contact in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)).

4. **Contribution terms (inbound license).** By submitting a contribution for
   inclusion, you agree to the terms in
   [`CONTRIBUTORS.md` → "Contributing terms"](CONTRIBUTORS.md): your contribution
   is licensed to the public under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, **and** you grant Serendip
   Commons Society a non-exclusive license to also use it commercially, so the
   Society can keep a single commercial-publishing path for the book as a whole.
   You retain copyright in your contribution. If you do not wish to grant the
   second part, please keep your work as your own CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 fork rather
   than submitting it here.

5. **Commercial use.** Forbidden by default; available only by separate license
   from Serendip Commons Society, on terms the Society sets — see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE).

The copyright holders retain full rights to their **own** material, so the
founder may publish a commercial edition and open-source the work, in either
order (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) → "Reservation of rights").
