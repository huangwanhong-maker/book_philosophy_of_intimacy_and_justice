## What it argues
What is wealth? — a gold ring, a bride-price, a shared life? The paper argues the answer is **ontological, not economic**: the *form of wealth* organising an intimate relation determines whether the parties can exist as **co-evolving** beings or are reduced to exchangeable units in a system of accumulation. It develops **Generative Relational Wealth (GRW)**: wealth generated through co-evolutionary activity in the relational field, belonging to the **between** rather than to either individual, whose defining property is that **use does not deplete but may augment** it — because GRW is stored not in any material or symbolic medium but in the permanent structural modifications of the relation's **attractor landscape**. GRW is the *self-fuel* of the relational field (the **generative relational cycle**: generated by co-evolution, it becomes the condition of further co-evolution).

It maps the major wealth-theory traditions (mercantilism, classical political economy, Marx's commodity fetishism, Federici's social reproduction, Sen's capabilities, Mauss's gift, Eglash's generative justice), showing what each illuminates and misses; then shows how traditional wealth forms (bride-price, gold, dowry) embed intimacy in accumulation, and how **three mechanisms — settlement, commodification, indebtedness — foreclose** genuine co-evolution. A central chapter analyses the **"happiness jar"** (couples recording and artistically processing shared moments) as a case study in GRW accumulation, depletion-resistance, and regeneration, drawing on semiotics (Peirce's index, Benjamin's aura), psychoanalysis (drive, the objet a), and theoretical physics (relational STDP, phase-space reconstruction, the Δ between symbolic and real), with a clearly-marked speculative conjecture that an operator's **non-zero relational remainder** is the source of GRW's inexhaustibility. Feminist, psychoanalytic, epistemic-justice, and Hegelian-Marxist perspectives argue GRW is **non-phallic wealth** bypassing the symbolic order's power structures, **care labour** its primary production, and the material/GRW relation dialectically irreducible. Extends Paper XIV (eudaimonics) and Paper IX (generativity) into **political economy**.

## Key concepts & coined terms
- **Generative Relational Wealth (GRW)** — wealth of the *between*, generated by co-evolution, **augmented (not depleted) by use**, stored in the relation's attractor landscape; the paper's central construct.
- **The generative relational cycle** — GRW as self-fuel: its use is the condition of further generation (cf. Papers IX/XI cycles).
- **Settlement / commodification / indebtedness** — the three mechanisms by which traditional wealth forms foreclose co-evolution.
- **The happiness jar** — case study: artistic processing of shared memories reignites a withering relational cycle.
- **Relational remainder** R(O(x), x) ≠ 0 — speculative source of GRW's inexhaustibility (marked as conjecture).
- **Non-phallic wealth** — GRW bypasses the symbolic order's power structures; care labour as its production mechanism.
