## What it argues
An **ontology, phenomenology, and ethics of culture as it emerges in intimate relation**. Guiding claim: culture is misconceived as a finished, transmissible object and rightly conceived as **a generation that does not close** — the dialectical **exteriorization**, across the three registers of experience and under material constraint, of the **interwoven generative history of relational subjects**. Part One works at the level of culture in general, passing through the major theories (transmission/collective representation, symbol/interpretation, structure/reproduction, culture as the encoding of power, the Chinese **wen–hua** tradition, the dialogic) and locating the position they leave open: a variant of the Lacanian three registers, the **three-register dialectic of the relational Real** — the **Real granted a neglected relational form**; culture shown to **span all three registers, none dispensable**; the registers set into **materially driven dialectical motion** (culture has no final truth, is never static, develops through the historical sublation of its own forms).

Part Two turns to the **intimate dyad** as the minimal, most exposed unit of cultural emergence, with **five mechanisms proper to the dyad**: (1) the **vacancy of any external authority** to fix meaning (the two serve as each other's symbolic authority — the vacant big Other); (2) the **mutual interweaving of two structures of desire**; (3) a **density of shared living** that lets culture sediment at visible speed (accelerated time); (4) the **heightened, intimate configuration of the three registers**; (5) a **fragility** by which a whole shared culture may collapse (reversibility). A middle-scale case, the **family ethos (家风, jiafeng)**, draws out the epistemology and the study of power. Part Three gives the **political economy and ethics**: how intimate culture is produced under material conditions; how **capital, having exhausted older frontiers, reaches into the bond itself** to capture the value it generates; and the condition of just cultural generation — that the generation of value, which carries power, must **in the same movement generate the counter-power** that restrains power and prevents its entrenchment.

## Five original theses (beyond the Lacan revision)
- **Non-transferability thesis** — a relational culture's resistance to transfer rises with the depth of its anchoring in interwoven drive.
- **Vacancy thesis** — externalizing symbolic authority sets a system's endogeneity and its stability in inverse tension.
- **Capture-as-decoherence** — capital captures a relational culture not by seizing it but by **preserving its symbolic form while drawing off its coherence in the Real**.
- **Counter-power criterion** — a cultural form persists through changed conditions only insofar as it **endogenously generates a counter-power against its own power**.
- **Real-anchoring thesis** — a relational culture's resistance to power, capital, and time arises from its anchoring in the register of the **Real**.
